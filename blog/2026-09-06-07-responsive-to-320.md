## What changed

The user asked, in one line, for the site to be responsive down to 320 pixels and then deployed. The calendar work had already left a narrow-screen breakpoint at 640px in `global.css`, so the real question was what breaks below it. Measured rather than guessed, the answer was two things:

- On the Anders Enqvist centerfold, the thesis banner set "COMMUNICATION:" in 28px Archivo Black. At 320px the banner has about 230px of text width, the word needs more, and it pushed the whole page one pixel sideways.
- On Yan Fang's defense page the title, still 28.5px at that width, ran fifteen lines, and "TRANSCRIPTOMICS" was wider than the column and clipped at the edge.

Everything else held at 320: the masthead and its three nav links on one line, the headline strip stacked, the filters, the toolbar wrapping into three rows, the day list, the week list, the month grid with its dots, the two-column year view, the about page.

The fix is commit `24ffac2`, stylesheets only:

- `body` gets `overflow-wrap: break-word` as the last resort: a word longer than its line breaks instead of pushing the page sideways.
- A small-phone step at 360px (`22.5rem`) in `global.css`: column padding drops from 1.25rem to 1rem, and the logo, the page title and the defense title each go one size down. The defense title also steps down at the existing 640px breakpoint, from 1.9rem to 1.6rem, since 28px display type never suited a phone.
- The same step in `centerfold.css`: the 24px paddings become 16px, the name, thesis and quote sizes come down, and the close-up facts stack the label over the value instead of sharing a row with a 96px label column. The two `auto-fit` grids now use `minmax(min(280px, 100%), 1fr)`, so a track can never be wider than a box narrower than 280px.
- Three small allowances: the masthead nav may wrap, the chip head (time and institution badge) may wrap, and the defense facts' value column has a zero minimum.

Then the push. The Deploy workflow (run 34054074922) validated, built, rsynced and smoke-checked in about thirty seconds, and the same audit run against `https://phdtv.net` reported the home page, the Enqvist centerfold and the Yan Fang page at a document width of 320 with nothing sticking out, at exactly the page heights of the local build.

## Why it matters

A TV guide is read on the sofa, on a phone, and 320 CSS pixels is the narrowest viewport still in common use: the first small iPhones, cheap Android phones, and any phone in split view. Print-inspired display type is exactly what breaks there. A heading set big enough to look like a magazine cover will eventually meet a word that does not fit, and a thesis title is where such words live: "transcriptomics", "socio-hydrological", "varhaiskasvatuksen" are all in the seed records. The site now degrades by stepping the type down and, failing that, by breaking the word, never by scrolling sideways.

## How it works

**The audit.** Nothing in the test suite can measure layout, so the measurement is a hundred-line Node script speaking the DevTools protocol to headless Chrome, with no dependencies beyond Node's own `fetch` and `WebSocket`. For each URL it opens a target, sets `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride` to the width under test (mobile, scale 2), navigates, waits for `document.fonts.ready` and a second more for the islands to hydrate and read their view from the URL, then walks every element in the body and reports any whose bounding box leaves the viewport or whose `scrollWidth` exceeds its `clientWidth` with overflow visible. It ends with `Page.captureScreenshot` beyond the viewport, so each run also leaves a full-page image to look at. The script is saved beside this entry as `audit.mjs`.

It ran over eleven URLs covering every page type: the home page, the day, week, month and year views, an empty period, two defense pages including the longest title in the records, both centerfolds and the about page.

| 641 | 5 | (not run) | clean, after one regression fix below |
| 1280 | 4 | (not run) | clean |

```
OVERFLOW  /defenses/2026/2026-09-24-wur-yan-fang/  vw=320 doc=320 body=320 offenders=4
    [text] <h1 class="defense-title">  left=19 right=301 w=283 "Diet, Liver Fat, and Metabolic Health: I"
```

**The measurement that lied first.** The first version of the script only compared bounding boxes with the viewport. It reported the centerfold as "document width 321, zero offenders" and the defense page as clean, and only the screenshots showed the truth: inline text that overflows its block does not widen the block's box, so a rect-only check cannot see it. The `scrollWidth` comparison is what made both pages fail properly before the fix, and pass after it.

**Two forms of word breaking.** The first fix used `overflow-wrap: anywhere` on `body`. At 320 it was fine; at 641px, the narrowest width at which the week view is still seven columns, the chips' time and badge, two flex items side by side, shrank and broke "11:30" into "11:3" and "0". The `anywhere` value counts the breaks it may add in an element's min-content size, so flex and grid items can shrink below their words. `break-word` breaks only after layout and leaves sizing alone, which is what a safety net should do. The chip head got `flex-wrap: wrap` on top, so at that width the badge drops under the time instead of poking out of the chip.

## What's next

- iOS Safari zooms into a `select` whose font is smaller than 16px, and the filters' selects are 13.5px. A `pointer: coarse` rule could set them to 16px on touch screens; it changes the look of the filter box, so it waits for the user.
- The toolbar buttons are 28px tall, under the usual 44px touch-target guidance, and the year view's day cells are smaller still. Not a width problem, but the next thing a phone user meets.
- The audit script lives in this entry's media folder. If it earns a third use it belongs in `scripts/` with an npm script, local only, since CI has no Chrome.
- The Deploy run still logs the Node 20 deprecation for `actions/checkout@v4` and `actions/setup-node@v4`.

## Surprises

An earlier entry recorded that headless Chrome will not open a window narrower than about 500px and suggested an iframe. Device-metrics emulation over the protocol is not bound by the window at all: the page lays out at a true 320px, with `innerWidth` and media queries to match, and no iframe is needed.

Chrome also exited on its own between runs, silently, several times; the script now spawns the browser itself and relaunches it when the socket drops, which turned a flaky tool into a boring one.

And a small one worth writing down: `rem` in a media query means the initial 16px, not the page's 15px root, so the existing `40rem` breakpoint is 640px (which is why `centerfold.css` pairs it with 639px), and the new small-phone step at `22.5rem` is 360px.
